The Box Top · Module 1

Lego sets come with instructions. A puzzle just gives you a picture. Once you find the four corners, you can outline the frame, and the other pieces get easier to place. The box top does three jobs: shows the finished picture, names the pieces, and shows you where to start.

Module 2

Corners One and Two

Where your money sits and what you want your money to do.

Module 2 covers Chapters 4 and 5. One idea: before you decide anything, know where your money sits and what you want it to do.

Corner One · Where your money sits

We want an axe, not a Swiss Army knife.

Your money should usually be earmarked for one or two big reasons.

The Box Top · Module 2

You could chop down a decent-sized tree with a Swiss Army knife. It would take all day. An axe has one job and does it well. Word track: Your money should usually be earmarked for one or two big reasons. We want an axe, not a Swiss Army knife.

The liquidity ladder

The rung tells you how fast you can get to it

Spend from the bottom up. Protect your least liquid money for last.

HARDEST TO REACH

An annuity or a business

Stocks and bonds

Home equity line or cash value life

Savings

Checking and cash on hand

EASIEST TO REACH

The Box Top · Module 2

Build the ladder from the bottom up. Word track: Most people think of their money as one big pile. It’s not. It sits on different rungs of a ladder, and the rung tells you how fast you can get to it. In a perfect world, you spend from the bottom up and protect your least liquid money for last.

Two ways to get it wrong

Too much at the bottom, or too little

Bottom-heavy

Too much sitting in checking and savings, doing nothing.

All in

Nearly everything invested, with very little cash on hand.

“If you weren’t lazy saving it, your savings shouldn’t be lazy either.”

The Box Top · Module 2

Tell the composite about the extra $60,000 in savings. Word track: That extra $60,000 wasn’t emergency money. It was fear of making the wrong move. If you weren’t lazy saving it, your savings shouldn’t be lazy either.

Corner Two · What you want your money to do

Every retirement decision runs through three levers

Time

Freedom and flexibility. What you traded for income.

Cash flow

A reliable stream of income for day-to-day life.

Cash

What you saved and stockpiled along the way.

In retirement you pull the time lever less, so cash has to support cash flow.

The Box Top · Module 2

Everyone starts with the same amount of time. You trade it for income, and what you save becomes cash. In retirement, the time lever gets pulled less, so cash has to support cash flow.

Cash flow doesn’t make itself. It takes time or cash to keep it going.

Word track: Cash flow doesn’t make itself. It takes either time or cash to keep it going. When you stop trading time, cash has to pick up the load.

Module 3

Corners Three and Four

What it costs in tax and how your money works.

Module 3 covers Chapters 6 and 7. One idea: the balance on your statement doesn’t tell the whole story. Every dollar carries a tax bill and a job.

Corner Three · What it costs in tax

Your statement says $400,000. How much of it is yours?

Qualified money is a future tax bill. You deferred it. You didn’t erase it. The train is coming.

The Box Top · Module 3

Hold up a statement and let them guess. Word track: Qualified money is a future tax bill. Think of it like a freight train coming down the track. You can plan for it or not, but the train is coming.

The three tax buckets

Every account fits in one of three buckets

Qualified

Not taxed yet. Taxed when it comes out.

401(k), 403(b), traditional IRA

Non-qualified

Already taxed.

Checking, savings, CDs, brokerage

Tax-privileged

Can come out tax-free when the rules are followed.

Roth accounts

Sort your accounts by tax status, not by balance.

The Box Top · Module 3 · Educational only, not tax advice

Word track: Sort your accounts by their tax status, not their balance. Every dollar you have carries a tax implication.

The product doesn’t decide the bucket. The tax status does.

A CD can sit in an IRA. Same product, different bucket.

Word track: Don’t let the product fool you. A CD can sit in an IRA. The product doesn’t decide the bucket. The tax status does.

Why the mix matters

Most retirees find most of their money in one bucket

Every withdrawal is taxable

when it all sits in qualified accounts.

Required distributions come later

whether you need the money or not.

Bring this to your CPA

Knowing your buckets makes that conversation better.

The Box Top · Module 3 · Educational only, not tax advice

Stop here and route it. Word track: I’m not your CPA, and this isn’t tax advice. The point is to see which bucket your money is in so you can have a better conversation with your tax professional. Tell the Dawsons from Chapter 6 if time allows, and route estate planning to an attorney.

Corner Four · How your money works

Every dollar has a job, whether you gave it one or not

Safety

Your principal is protected. What you put in, you can’t lose.

Growth

It can outpace inflation. If it doesn’t, it’s losing value.

Access

You can get to it quickly when you need it.

Corner One shows where your money sits. Corner Four shows what it’s doing.

The Box Top · Module 3

Corner One is location. Corner Four is the job. Walk the three modes, then move to the balance slide.

The goal isn’t to pick one. It’s the right balance for where you are now.

Not where you were when you opened the account.

Word track: If everything’s safe, it may not grow enough. If everything’s growing, it may not be safe enough. That’s the balance we’re looking for.
